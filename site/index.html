Critical care (London, England)Grondman I, Koeken VACM, Couwenbergh T et al.DOIPubMedPMC 全文编号 199
血液
原文结论

Our findings shed light on the heterogeneous immune landscape underlying sepsis and provide opportunities for patient stratification for future therapeutic development.

完整摘要（5 段）

BackgroundSepsis is a highly heterogeneous syndrome characterized by variable immune dysregulation states, including hyperinflammation and immunosuppression. Previous immunotherapy attempts in sepsis have largely failed, likely due to a "one-size-fits-all" approach that ignores each patient's immune status. The recent ImmunoSep randomized clinical trial demonstrated that precision immunotherapy guided by the presence of either macrophage activation-like syndrome (MALS) or immune paralysis can improve early organ dysfunction in sepsis patients. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying these immune endotypes remain unclear.

ObjectivesTo identify the immunological signatures that distinguish MALS and immune paralysis.

MethodsWe used single-cell RNA sequencing to profile circulating leukocytes of 6 healthy controls and 16 sepsis patients classified as MALS, immune paralysis or unclassified (when criteria for neither of these two immune endotypes were applicable). Classification was based on surrogate biomarkers ferritin and HLA-DR expression on monocytes. Thereafter, the transcriptional programs of these groups were compared.

ResultsPronounced differences were detected mainly in the transcriptional signature of monocytes from these patients, with a clear distinction between MALS and immune paralysis. Unsupervised clustering analysis revealed the existence of MALS-specific monocyte clusters, as well as one sepsis-specific monocyte cluster that was linked to greater comorbidity burden and may reflect increased clinical vulnerability in sepsis. These findings were validated in two independent cohorts, in which urosepsis was characterized by heterogeneous MALS and immune paralysis monocyte signatures. Moreover, MALS-specific monocyte clusters showed overlapping transcriptional signatures with severe COVID-19.

ConclusionsOur findings shed light on the heterogeneous immune landscape underlying sepsis and provide opportunities for patient stratification for future therapeutic development.

Diagnostic microbiology and infectious diseaseNaderi M, Sadidi R, Besharati R et al.DOIPubMed编号 200
血液综述
摘要末段

完整摘要

Obligate intracellular bacteria (Rickettsia, Orientia, Ehrlichia, Anaplasma, Coxiella, Chlamydia) and cell wall-deficient Mycoplasma species present profound diagnostic challenges due to their fastidious growth, low circulating burden, and genomic instability. Nucleic Acid Amplification Tests (NAATs) have revolutionized detection, yet critical gaps persist in distinguishing active infection from persistent DNA, standardizing cycle threshold interpretation, and integrating host-response biomarkers. This structured narrative review provides a comprehensive analysis of the complete diagnostic armamentarium for these pathogens. It is based on a reproducible, non-systematic literature survey and is not a PRISMA-compliant systematic review. We cover conventional methods (culture, serology), NAAT platforms (qPCR, digital PCR, isothermal amplification), and emerging technologies including CRISPR-Cas diagnostics, metagenomic sequencing, nanopore sequencing, biosensors, and host-response biomarker profiling. We introduce four conceptual frameworks: the "biological noise" paradigm reframing low-copy detection as a signal-to-noise challenge; a temporal-dynamic algorithm accounting for pathogen-specific detection windows; the "detection-disease disconnect" emphasizing clinical contextualization of NAAT positivity; and multi-analyte integration combining NAAT with viability markers and host inflammatory signatures. We also position these frameworks relative to prior diagnostic-interpretation theories, including STARD, QUADAS-2, MIQE, Bayesian pretest-posttest reasoning, Ct-value interpretation guidance, and host-response classifier frameworks. We compare ten clinically significant pathogens across technical and biological parameters, evaluate point-of-care deployment strategies, and address antimicrobial resistance testing. Performance metrics are presented as context-dependent estimates rather than fixed constants. The next frontier lies not in incremental assay improvements but in system-level integration of pathogen detection, viability assessment, host-response profiling, and clinical phenotyping, supported by artificial intelligence-enhanced interpretation and global standardization efforts.

原文结论

A five-gene host-response signature, conserved in whole blood and plasma cfRNA, reliably distinguishes active tuberculosis from latent infection and healthy controls, enabling minimal, cross-platform diagnostics.

完整摘要（5 段）

BackgroundTuberculosis (TB) remains one of the deadliest infectious diseases worldwide, with gaps in timely, accurate diagnosis sustaining transmission and disproportionate burden in low- and middle-income countries. Current sputum-based tests show limited sensitivity and poorly distinguish active TB (ATB) from latent infection (LTBI), highlighting the need for reliable non-sputum biomarkers reflecting host disease status.

ObjectiveTo investigate the conservation of tuberculosis-associated host transcriptomic signals between plasma cell-free RNA and whole blood, and to identify a minimal gene signature for accurate disease classification.

MethodsWe applied an integrated transcriptomic and machine learning framework to discover a biologically plausible minimal gene signature associated with ATB. RNA sequencing data of whole blood, and plasma cell-free RNA available publicly were analysed independently by DESeq2 to determine differentially expressed genes among ATB, LTBI, and healthy controls. Core disease-associated pathways were determined using functional enrichment and protein-protein interaction network analyses. Genes were prioritized based on biological relevance, pathway convergence, and cross-dataset consistency. The resulting gene signature was used to train the machine learning classifiers to evaluate diagnostic performance.

ResultsTranscriptomic and network analyses revealed that ATB is characterized by interferon-driven immune activation, neutrophil-mediated inflammation, and dysregulated immune regulation, detectable in both whole blood and cfRNA. A five-gene host signature (GBP5, GBP1, ANKRD22, CCR5AS, and KLF2) demonstrated robust diagnostic performance in both whole blood and cfRNA, capturing shared host-response biology despite compartment-specific expression differences. Machine learning models trained on this gene set achieved high diagnostic accuracy, with area under the receiver operating characteristic curve values of 0.98 in whole blood and 0.94 in cfRNA. Notably, CCR5AS, a long non-coding RNA, emerged as a previously underexplored component of host transcriptional responses in TB.

ConclusionsA five-gene host-response signature, conserved in whole blood and plasma cfRNA, reliably distinguishes active tuberculosis from latent infection and healthy controls, enabling minimal, cross-platform diagnostics.

The accuracy of PCR compared to metagenomic next-generation sequencing (mNGS) for diagnosis of IMD remains unknown. Plasma samples from patients with proven and probable IMD and no IMD were tested with mold PCR and shotgun mNGS. Mold PCR detected Aspergillus species, Mucorales agents, Fusarium species, Scedosporium species, and Lomentospora prolificans. The sensitivity and specificity of mold PCR and mNGS were measured. The median PCR cycle threshold was 40.6 (IQR, 37.5-41.5) in PCR-positive mNGS-negative cases compared with 37.3 (IQR, 34.5-39.3; P = 0.004) in PCR-positive mNGS-positive cases. ImportanceInvasive mold disease (IMD) is a major cause of morbidity and mortality in immunocompromised patients. Early diagnosis and initiation of tailored antifungal therapy are critical for optimizing outcomes in patients with IMD. Detection of circulating fungal cell-free DNA (cfDNA) in the acellular fraction of blood is a novel testing modality that is increasingly used for early and non-invasive diagnosis of IMD. This study represents the first head-to-head accuracy study to compare the sensitivity and specificity of PCR with mNGS for the diagnosis of IMD. The study showed that the overall mold PCR was significantly more sensitive compared with mNGS, while both assays demonstrated very high specificity. Similar trends were observed in a smaller subgroup of patients with invasive aspergillosis, mucormycosis, and scedosporiosis. 完整摘要（4 段）

BackgroundCentral neuropsychiatric systemic lupus erythematosus (cNPSLE) is a major cause of mortality in systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE). Distinguishing cNPSLE from central nervous system (CNS) infections remains a critical clinical challenge due to overlapping clinical manifestations, often leading to diagnostic delays and inappropriate therapeutic interventions. We aimed to identify and validate cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) biomarkers to resolve this diagnostic dilemma.

MethodsWe conducted the largest cNPSLE CSF proteomic investigation to date using high-throughput data-independent acquisition mass spectrometry (DIA-MS) in a discovery cohort comprising 20 patients with cNPSLE, 8 with CNS infection (cNIC), and 11 non-diseased controls (NDC). Differentially expressed proteins were analyzed for pathway enrichment. Candidate biomarkers were subsequently validated using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) in two independent cohorts: an internal validation cohort (46 cNPSLE vs. 29 cNIC) and an external validation cohort (40 cNPSLE vs. 13 cNIC). Robustness of discovery-stage protein selection was assessed by stratified bootstrap resampling and leave-one-out analysis. ResultsOur proteomic profiling quantified 2175 CSF proteins, revealing enrichment in immune and metabolic pathways. ConclusionWe identified and validated a three-protein CSF panel (MATN3, MERTK, and VSIG4) that differentiates cNPSLE from CNS infections across multi-center cohorts. This diagnostic signature holds potential for clinical translation to facilitate early diagnosis and precise management of NPSLE.

TREM-1 in Sepsis and Septic Shock: From Pathophysiology to Precision Immunomodulation.↗

Seminars in respiratory and critical care medicineEvrard B, Van Der Poll T, Chousterman B et al.DOIPubMed编号 193
血液

完整摘要

Sepsis remains one of the leading causes of death worldwide, and decades of immunomodulatory trials targeting inflammation have failed to improve outcomes, largely because of the biological heterogeneity of the host response. Triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells-1 (TREM-1) has emerged as a particularly relevant pathway in this context: rather than initiating pathogen recognition, TREM-1 amplifies signals generated by pattern-recognition receptors, acting as a rheostat that fine-tunes the magnitude of the innate immune response to promote antimicrobial defense while limiting inflammation-induced tissue injury. Both insufficient and excessive activation of this pathway have been associated with adverse outcomes in experimental and clinical studies. Soluble TREM-1 (sTREM-1), released proportionally to pathway activation, has been extensively investigated as a biomarker of infection and disease severity. Its diagnostic performance is moderate and best interpreted alongside clinical and microbiological data, but its prognostic value is more consistent, particularly when assessed serially or combined with complementary host-response markers. More recently, sTREM-1 has been explored as a theranostic biomarker to guide pathway-directed treatment. Nangibotide, the most clinically advanced TREM-1 inhibitor, has demonstrated feasibility and short-term safety across phase 1 and phase 2 trials, but has not yet met its primary efficacy endpoint in an unselected population; exploratory analyses suggest that treatment benefit may be concentrated among patients with the highest baseline sTREM-1 concentrations, a hypothesis that remains to be prospectively validated. This review summarizes the biology of the TREM-1 pathway, the clinical evidence supporting sTREM-1 as a diagnostic, prognostic, and theranostic biomarker, and the current state of TREM-1-targeted therapeutics. It also discusses what the clinical development of nangibotide illustrates about the hope and the difficulty of precision immunomodulation in sepsis.
