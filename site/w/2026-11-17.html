原文结论

完整摘要（4 段）

BackgroundCirculating plasma microbial DNA (mDNA) is a promising biomarker for sepsis, but its accuracy is hindered by the low abundance of pathogen DNA and the presence of contaminants and commensals. mDNA enrichment using a selection of short fragments and single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) library preparation can improve pathogen yield but also increases background noise. Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) remains a leading cause of hospital and intensive care admission and infectious death in Europe, particularly among older adults, people with multimorbidity, and immunocompromised patients. 完整摘要（2 段）

Purpose of reviewTimely and accurate diagnosis of central nervous system (CNS) infections remains challenging because of nonspecific clinical characteristics and limited diagnostic accuracy of conventional tests. Novel cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) biomarkers may improve early diagnosis and facilitate differentiation between infectious aetiologies. This review summarizes established and emerging CSF biomarkers for the diagnosis of CNS infections, with a focus on bacterial meningitis, and discusses their potential clinical application.

Recent findingsSeveral novel CSF biomarkers have demonstrated excellent diagnostic performance for bacterial meningitis. Among these, CSF C-reactive protein (CRP) has recently been implemented in clinical practice, while heparin-binding protein and combinations of CSF CRP with inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and IL-1β show similarly high diagnostic accuracy. Pathogen-specific biomarkers, including CXCL13 for neuroborreliosis and β-D-glucan and galactomannan for fungal CNS infections, further expand the diagnostic repertoire. In addition, multi-omics approaches, including metabolomics, lipidomics, and proteomics, have identified promising candidates such as pyruvate and phosphatidylcholines, although these require further validation. Untargeted techniques, including metagenomic next-generation sequencing and phage immunoprecipitation sequencing, further enhance pathogen detection and may complement microbiological testing. Novel biomarkers have the potential to substantially improve the diagnosis of CNS infections. Several biomarkers for bacterial meningitis are already available for clinical implementation, particularly in patients with intermediate CSF leukocyte counts, where diagnostic uncertainty is greatest. For viral and other CNS infections biomarkers remain scarce. Future studies should focus on validation of emerging biomarkers and on integrating biomarker panels with advanced molecular diagnostic techniques to enable rapid, accurate and clinically applicable diagnostic strategies.

完整摘要（5 段）

IntroductionAcute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), frequently triggered by sepsis, is associated with high mortality and severe disruption of the alveolar-capillary barrier driven by immune dysregulation. This study aimed to characterize immune-stromal crosstalk in sepsis-induced ARDS and develop a predictive risk model.

MethodsBulk transcriptomic datasets were used for differential expression analysis, immune deconvolution, and development of a sepsis-to-ARDS risk model. eQTL-based Mendelian randomization evaluated associations between genetically predicted gene expression and sepsis susceptibility. Human and mouse single-cell datasets were used for pseudotime and inferred ligand-receptor communication analyses.

ResultsTen core genes, including CD52 and MACF1, were prioritized by integrating ARDSassociated differential expression with genetic associations for sepsis susceptibility. CD52 showed dynamic expression changes in CD8+ T cells. CD52-low states were associated with stronger inferred MIF- and TGF-β-related communication and with transcriptional programs linked to immune recruitment and barrier dysfunction. The four-gene signature showed moderate discrimination in the external-validation cohort (AUC = 0.688).

DiscussionThese findings identify CD52 as a marker of a dynamic immunoregulatory state in sepsis- induced ARDS and associate CD52-low states with altered inferred T cell-stromal communication. The four-gene signature provides proof-of-concept support for transcriptomic risk stratification but requires further clinical validation.

ConclusionCD52 marks a time-dependent immune state associated with inferred T cell-stromal communication in sepsis-induced ARDS. The four-gene signature provides proof-of-concept support for risk stratification, while functional studies are required to determine whether CD52 directly modifies barrier-related signaling.

完整摘要

Hepatitis of unknown origin (HUO) remains a diagnostic challenge, with metagenomic studies underexplored in adult cohorts. This study characterized the plasma and fecal virome in a predominantly adult HUO cohort (48 adults and 1 adolescent). A total of 59 samples (44 plasma, 15 fecal) from 49 patients were subjected to ultra-multiplex targeted ribonucleic acid (RNA) sequencing (139 RNA viruses) and unbiased metagenomic sequencing. Targeted sequencing detected HCV in one plasma sample, confirmed by follow-up reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) and seroconversion, leading to case reclassification. Metagenomic sequencing identified HBV sequences in another patient, along with sporadic goose circovirus (GoCV) and an unconfirmed low-level influenza A-like signal. Viral community analysis revealed distinct niche-specific profiles between plasma and feces. Overall, this exploratory study failed to identify a common pathogenic agent, underscoring the etiological heterogeneity of HUO. The detection of known hepatotropic viruses highlights the diagnostic value of metagenomic sequencing in complementing conventional serology and reclassifying some HUO cases. Larger controlled studies are warranted.
